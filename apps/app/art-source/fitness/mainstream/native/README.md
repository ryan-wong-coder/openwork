# Three exact exercise demonstrations

| Canonical ID and name | Public stem | Kind |

| `0417` `dumbbell standing calf raise` | `calf-raise` | Zeno mascot |

All six final files are in `apps/app/public/zeno/fitness/`. Each stem has an MP4
and `-poster.webp`. Use this directory's **`provenance.json`** as the combined
three-entry import; the two per-exercise provenance files are intermediate
records and must not be imported a second time. `validation.json` records actual
whole-file hashes, format probes and the scope of the local checks.

## Mascot generation

Built-in Image Gen produced the selected `calf-raise-v2-reference.png` and
`hip-thrust-v2-reference.png`. The sole image input was Zeno's own
`curl-reference.png`, used for character identity. The full final image prompts
are in the matching `*-v2-image.json` files. Official LibTV CLI 1.1.1 generated
each selected video using Seedance 2.0 VIP, one image reference, 720p, 16:9,
eight seconds and sound off. Matching `*-v2-video-request.json` and
`*-v2-video-generation.json` preserve final prompts, actual node/task IDs and
settings. Uploads, generation and downloads all used the official CLI.

The selected calf video keeps the full head and feet visible, with both heels
raising and lowering while toes stay on the floor and dumbbells remain at the
sides. The selected hip video keeps shoulders against the bench, feet planted,
and the padded bar attached to the hip crease throughout lowering and extension.
Both original clips were encoded as silent H.264/yuv420p, 1024×576, CRF26,
with fast-start metadata. Posters use cwebp q86 at 1024 pixels wide.

## Lunge diagram

Both mascot lunge attempts were rejected. The selected lunge is generated by
`lunge-diagram.mjs`, an original vector drawing adapted from Zeno's existing
`0612` scene. It has separate forward-step, lowering, hold, rising and return
phases. The rear toe is anchored throughout, the leading foot remains planted
during lowering and rising, and the rear foot points forward with its heel
raised at the bottom. Frame generation checks these contact constraints and
all joint bounds. It produces 192 frames at 24 fps for an exact eight-second
loop, rasterized with Sharp and encoded as H.264/yuv420p CRF26 without audio.
The SVG generator and its starting/lowered SVGs are preserved here.

To reproduce with a local installation of Sharp, ffmpeg and cwebp:

```sh
node apps/app/art-source/fitness/mainstream/native/lunge-diagram.mjs /absolute/output/directory
```

`ZENO_RENDER_NODE_MODULES` can point to a bundled `node_modules` directory that
contains Sharp. The output is a standalone render; it does not launch a browser,
run a model or contact a service.

## Review and limits

The final three videos were fully decoded, their eight-frame contact sheets
were actually viewed, and all three final WebP posters were viewed. Each video
has one H.264 stream, no audio, 1024×576 dimensions and approximately eight
seconds of duration. This is local source-media QA; it does not establish a
published webpage, packaged desktop playback or professional technique testing.

`rejected-attempts.json` records four rejected generated clips and their reasons.
Small rejected contact sheets preserve the review evidence. Rejected full raster
references were removed from the workspace; original downloaded videos were
removed after hashing. The public directory contains only the three accepted
pairs. There were six LibTV video generations in total, with one retry per
exercise and no further paid attempts.

The movement brief used the catalogue's exact identity and equipment. Forward
stepping, controlled lowering and returning were checked against the text of
[ACE's forward lunge guide](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/94/forward-lunge/).
Straight-knee calf movement and controlled lowering were checked against
[NASM's calf training discussion](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/training/calf-training-how-to-program-this-stubborn-muscle-group-for-clients).
No images or video from these sources, openGym or other exercise-media libraries
were copied or supplied to generation. These assets illustrate movement; no
clinical, safety, efficacy or professional human technique validation is claimed.
